# Bundle A scene: "Last Service"

One emotionally intense original scene, written to stress-test Dubbing v2
emotion transfer: a whisper open, warmth, a laugh caught mid-grief, a voice
break, a defiant crescendo, and a whispered button. Every beat is a different
emotional register the dub has to carry across languages.

Performed as ONE continuous take (breaths and pauses are part of the
performance; dubbing preserves what exists in the source). Voice: Mitchell
retake 2026-07-12 IVC, eleven_v3 with performance tags.

## The monologue (as sent to TTS, tags included)

[softly] Thirty one years ago, my mother opened this restaurant with two pans, a folding table, and a recipe she refused to write down. [sighs] I grew up in this kitchen. I learned to walk on this floor. [laughs] I burned my first sauce right there... and she made me eat it anyway. [pause] Last month, the landlord called. And tonight... tonight we serve the last plate. [sad] Every scratch on this counter is a night we fed someone. Every chip, in every bowl. [determined] So no. We are not going quietly. We are going loud. One more service. Every burner lit. [softly] And when the last guest leaves, we turn off the lights the way she taught me. [whispers] One at a time.

## Why this scene for the dub demo

- The emotional dynamics (whisper, laugh through grief, break, crescendo,
  whisper) are exactly what traditional dubbing flattens and what Dubbing v2
  claims to preserve.
- Food and family translate in every target market (ES, DE, FR, PT).
- The verbs are shootable literally per Craft Law: opening the restaurant,
  burning the sauce, the phone call, serving plates, lighting burners,
  turning off lights one at a time.
- Diners at tables give the spectator intercut the law requires.
